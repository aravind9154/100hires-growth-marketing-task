# 1. AI-Powered SEO Content Production Playbook (SOP)

## Step 1: Define the Content Objective

Before I use AI, I define the purpose of the content. Every article should have a single objective that supports a business goal instead of being created simply to target a keyword or increase publishing volume. A clear objective helps me decide what the content should achieve, who it is intended for, and how I will measure its success.

Next, I identify the primary search intent behind the topic. Whether the user wants to learn, compare, evaluate, or make a purchase, the content must satisfy that intent from the beginning. If the objective and search intent are unclear, I do not proceed to the next stage because the quality of the final content depends on getting these fundamentals right.

I also choose topics based on business value and user needs rather than search volume alone. AI helps produce content efficiently, but the topic itself must contribute to meaningful business outcomes and provide value to the intended audience.

- Define one primary business objective before starting AI-assisted content production.

  ## Step 2: Conduct Keyword and Search Intent Research

After defining the objective, I research the topic before generating any content with AI. I identify keywords that align with the business objective and analyze what users expect to find when they search for those queries. This helps me create content that satisfies user intent instead of producing generic AI-generated articles.

I also group related keywords into topical clusters rather than treating every keyword as a separate article. This reduces content duplication, strengthens topical authority, and creates a more organized content strategy. To better understand user needs, I review search results, competitor pages, and community discussions to identify common questions, terminology, and content gaps.

  ## Step 3: Build the Topical Content Plan

Before generating content with AI, I organize the topic into a structured content plan. Instead of publishing unrelated articles, I create topical clusters where each page supports a broader subject. This helps search engines and AI systems understand the relationship between topics while strengthening topical authority across the website.

Each article has a clear purpose within the cluster and covers a unique aspect of the topic. I also plan internal linking between related pages so users and search engines can easily navigate the content ecosystem.

## Disagreement 1: Should AI Scale Content Production or Support Human Expertise?

### Author A: Matt Diggity

Matt Diggity recommends using AI to accelerate SEO workflows. He shows that AI can assist with keyword research, topical mapping, article outlines, first drafts, semantic optimization, content promotion, and programmatic SEO. His focus is improving production efficiency while keeping humans involved in the review process.

**Sources**

- Matt Diggity, *How ChatGPT Has Changed SEO Forever*, YouTube, published 3 years ago, research collected on **16/06/2026**  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eI2JREOaxgc

- Matt Diggity, *Programmatic SEO: Step-by-Step Case Study*, YouTube, published 2 years ago, research collected on **16/06/2026**  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DgWUgtZGqQ

### Author B: Marie Haynes and Lily Ray

Marie Haynes and Lily Ray recommend using AI only as a writing assistant. They emphasize that factual research, first-hand experience, expert review, and originality should remain human responsibilities because AI can generate inaccurate or unverified information.

**Sources**

- Marie Haynes, *AI & Search: What SEOs Need to Know (and Do) Right Now*, YouTube, research collected on **16/06/2026**

- Marie Haynes, *Discussing Google Bard: The Implications for SEO and Digital Marketing*, YouTube, research collected on **16/06/2026**

- Lily Ray, *You Are What You EEAT*, YouTube, research collected on **16/06/2026**

### Which Side I Take and Why

I agree with both approaches to some extent. AI should improve efficiency by helping with research organization, outlines, and first drafts, but every article should still be reviewed, fact-checked, and improved by a human before publication. This provides the speed benefits of AI without reducing content quality or trust.

### Which Side I Take and Why

I believe entity authority will become the stronger long-term advantage. Backlinks are still important because they help build trust and discovery, but they are only one signal. # 3. What I Rejected and Why

## Idea 1: Using Expired Domains and 301 Redirects to Accelerate Authority

Matt Diggity explains that expired domains and 301 redirects can reduce the time required to build authority for a new website. While these techniques may work in some situations, I decided not to include them in this playbook.

My goal was to create an SOP that any business can apply using sustainable SEO practices. Strategies that depend on purchasing expired domains or transferring existing authority are not practical for every organization and introduce additional risk. Instead, I chose to focus on building authority through high-quality content, topical coverage, and consistent publishing.

  # 4. My Original Idea

## Add an "AI Retrieval Review" Before Publishing

None of the experts I researched recommend reviewing content specifically from the perspective of an AI retrieval system before publication. Most experts discuss semantic SEO, entities, E-E-A-T, topical authority, and AI visibility, but I did not find a dedicated quality check that evaluates whether a page is easy for AI systems to retrieve and reference.

I propose adding an **AI Retrieval Review** as the final step before publishing. During this review, I would check whether the page answers the main question clearly, presents important information in structured sections, explains entities consistently, and makes key facts easy to identify without requiring AI systems to interpret long paragraphs.

I believe this could improve AI visibility because retrieval systems are designed to identify relevant and trustworthy passages quickly. Content that is well-structured and easy to extract may have a better chance of being selected for AI-generated responses while remaining useful for human readers.


# 5. Weaknesses of This Playbook

## 1. AI Search Is Still Evolving

Many recommendations in this playbook are based on current observations of AI-powered search systems. As Google AI Search, ChatGPT, Gemini, and other AI platforms continue to evolve, some recommendations may become less effective over time.

## 2. Limited Long-Term Evidence

Several concepts included in this playbook, such as AI visibility optimization and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), are relatively new. While multiple experts discuss these topics, there is limited long-term evidence showing which techniques consistently produce the best results.

## 3. Not Every Recommendation Was Independently Tested

This playbook is based on research collected from industry experts rather than my own large-scale experiments. Although I compared different viewpoints before making decisions, I have not personally validated every recommendation through long-term implementation.

## 4. The Playbook Assumes Basic SEO Knowledge

The workflow assumes that the reader understands fundamental SEO concepts such as search intent, keyword research, technical SEO, and content optimization. Beginners may require additional learning before applying every step effectively.

## 5. Results Will Depend on Execution

Following this SOP does not guarantee higher rankings or AI visibility. Success also depends on factors outside this playbook, including competition, website authority, content quality, and future search engine changes.
